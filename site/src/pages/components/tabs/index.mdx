# Tabs

Tabs are used to quickly navigate between views within the same context, allowing a user to access different contents without leaving the page.

## Best practices

- Labels are concise and use one to two words maximum.
- Use Tabs when the content and structure of the pages are different.
- A maximum of six tabs may be displayed. This is to maintain an uncluttered UI and reduce cognitive load for users.
- Tabs with related content should be grouped adjacent to each other.
- It's not recommended to use Tabs as filters.

## Vertical tabs and panels

`orientation='vertical'` stacks the tabs in a column beside their content, which fits many tabs or long labels, such as a settings page. `TabPanel` holds the content of a tab: give it an `id` that the `TabItem` points to with `aria-controls`, point back to the tab with `aria-labelledby`, and pass `active` to the panel of the active tab.

## Accessibility

The tabs form a `tablist`, and each `TabItem` tells whether it is selected (`aria-selected`) or disabled. Tab enters the list on the active tab, and the arrow keys, Home and End move between the tabs. A `TabPanel` can take the focus with Tab, so keyboard users reach its content even when it has no links or fields.
